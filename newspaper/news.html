World Exclusive

Nations Agree to Historic Net-Zero Pact in Geneva Summit

By Sarah Mitchell, James Okonkwo · Geneva Bureau

Over 180 countries have signed a landmark agreement pledging binding carbon-neutral targets by 2040 — the most ambitious climate deal in history.

In a dramatic turn of events late Thursday evening, delegates from 183 nations gathered in the grand hall of the Palais des Nations in Geneva signed what analysts are calling the most consequential environmental accord since the Paris Agreement.

The landmark deal, negotiated over four gruelling days of talks, commits signatories to achieving net-zero carbon emissions by 2040 — a decade ahead of previous targets — and establishes a $500 billion Green Transition Fund to assist developing nations in retrofitting their energy infrastructure.

"This is the moment the world chose survival over convenience," said UN Secretary-General in a press briefing that concluded just past midnight local time. Markets responded positively to the news, with green energy stocks surging more than seven percent at opening.

"History will mark this day as the turning point — the moment humanity chose a different path."

— UN Secretary-General, Geneva, March 8
